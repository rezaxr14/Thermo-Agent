AI data centers are adding on-site gas generation, and the heat it leaves is low grade.
Choosing the plant layout, keeping a plant model physically consistent and locating the exergy
losses are expert tasks, and a plant model can solve and still violate the second law.
We build a second-law-verified environment for these tasks: a physics-based simulator with
\val{env.n_components} component models and exergy accounting against an explicit dead state,
and eight verifiers whose checks are an exact, cheap reward. A small open model
(Qwen3.5-4B) is to be trained with LoRA supervised fine-tuning on programmatically verified
answers and then with GRPO against the verifiers, on four task families with structural
out-of-distribution splits: plant Repair, Design, exergy Audit and Ledger repair. All results
are simulation-driven: measured load and coolant traces set operating points of a physics model
with assumed technology constants, and no measured on-site-generation data center is involved.
Three model-independent simulator findings come from small samples. Of \val{led.n} generated
ledger rows with injected equation faults, \val{led.any_pct}\% show a closure residual or a
negative exergy destruction, and parameter-range checks see none. A first-law ranking of exergy
destroyers, prime mover excluded, finds the true top destroyer in \val{aud.fl_hits_ood} of
\val{aud.n_ood} held-out plants. Exhaust-only recovery limits a gas engine to about
\val{mech.engine_max_frac_pct}\% of the IT heat for absorption cooling.
