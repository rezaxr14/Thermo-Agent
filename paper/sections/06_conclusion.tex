\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We described a second-law-verified environment for on-site power, cooling and heat recovery in AI data centers, four task families with structural out-of-distribution splits, and a two-stage training recipe: supervised fine-tuning on a verifier-gated corpus of paraphrased traces, a catalog of plant diagrams, accepted frontier answers and a retrievable success-and-failure memory, followed by GRPO with the verifiers as the reward. The contribution is not a new optimizer but a setting in which the reward is the physics itself: an answer is accepted when mass, energy, second-law and site checks pass, and every attempt is kept so that failures and verified solutions teach the next answer.

The parts that are already measured are model-independent. A blind search repairs most injected faults but pays for it in solver calls; enumerating defaults and polishing reaches the reference design only after many evaluations; a first-law ranking mislocates exergy destruction on one sample and agrees with the second-law ranking on another; equation-level faults in an exergy ledger are often invisible to range checks. These findings do not depend on a language model, and they set the baselines and the difficulty of the task families.

The model-dependent claims remain open. The paper defines a frozen protocol with eight questions, a multi-arm comparison that includes our trained 4B model, zero-shot frontier models, frontier models with our inference text and with our physics tools, and other models, together with paired statistics, cost accounting and a reproducible trace figure. The corresponding tables are generated from those runs when they exist; until then we report the schema and the empty result asset rather than a number. When the runs are complete we will claim only what the protocol can support: sample efficiency in solver calls or design evaluations, transfer to held-out diagram combinations, and the value of the harness and the failure memory --- not real-plant validation and not an optimum that beats numerical search.
