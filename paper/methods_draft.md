# Methods (draft, written from the implementation)

All symbols match the code. Molar basis internally (J/mol, mol/s); SI units. Constants marked [assumed] are model settings, not measurements.

## 1. Plant model
A plant is a typed graph of components joined by streams. Streams are either ideal-gas mixtures (`CGAM_AIR`, `FLUE`, `CH4`) or CoolProp fluids (water, R245fa, cyclopentane, toluene). Components are solved sequentially; a loop is closed by a tear stream that carries fixed values, and the producing component must reproduce them (otherwise the solver reports non-convergence).

**Gas properties.** NASA-7 polynomials from GRI-Mech 3.0 for N2, O2, CO2, H2O(g) and CH4; enthalpy includes the formation enthalpy. Mixture entropy uses partial pressures, s = sum_i y_i s_i(T, y_i P). Air has the CGAM composition (N2 0.7748, O2 0.2059, CO2 0.0003, H2O 0.019).

**Combustor.** With lambda = fuel/air mole ratio, products are CO2 (y_CO2,air + lambda), H2O (y_H2O,air + 2 lambda), O2 (y_O2,air - 2 lambda), N2, each divided by 1 + lambda. Lambda solves (1 + lambda) h_p(T_out) = h_air + lambda h_F - q_loss lambda LHV, with LHV = h_CH4 + 2 h_O2 - h_CO2 - 2 h_H2O(g) at 298.15 K from the same polynomials.

**Turbomachinery.** Isentropic outlet at the specified outlet pressure, h_out = h_in -/+ (h_in - h_s)/eta_s for expansion/compression, work n (h_in - h_out). Pumps and compressors use h_out = h_in + (h_s - h_in)/eta_s.

**Gas engine.** Fuel n_F = P_el / (eta_el LHV); air n_a = a * 2 n_F / y_O2 with excess factor a. Exhaust enthalpy h_ex = (n_a h_air + n_F h_F - P_el - f_loss n_F LHV)/(n_a + n_F); exhaust temperature follows. Jacket and radiation heat f_loss n_F LHV is rejected to ambient, not recovered.

**Heat-transfer components.** Recuperators fix the cold outlet temperature and compute the hot outlet from the duty. The HRSG produces saturated steam; its pinch is the gas temperature at the start of evaporation minus the saturation temperature. The ORC evaporator places the pinch (pinch_K) at the bubble point T_b: h_3 = h_b + n_g (h_in - h_g(T_b + pinch)) / n_o. When the working-fluid flow is not given it lies in a window, n_max = n_g (h_g(T_b + pinch) - h_g(T_floor)) / (h_b - h_pump,out) (exhaust stays above T_floor) and n_min = n_g (h_g,in - h_g(T_b + pinch)) / (h(T_lim, P) - h_b) (vapour stays at or below T_lim), n_o = n_min + f (n_max - n_min); if n_min > n_max the exhaust is too hot for that fluid. T_lim is 420, 480, 560 K for R245fa, cyclopentane, toluene [assumed].

**Chillers and heat pump.** With log-mean temperatures T_lm(a, b) = (a - b)/ln(a/b) of the stream pairs:
- Absorption: COP = min(eps COP_rev, COP_max), COP_rev = (1 - T_r/T_g)/(T_r/T_e - 1), eps = 0.27, COP_max = 0.8 [assumed; measured single-effect units run at COP 0.55 to 0.70]. Generator duty Q_g = Q_e / COP; rejected heat Q_g + Q_e.
- Electric chiller: COP = eps (T_e - a)/((T_r + a) - (T_e - a)), eps = 0.5, approach a = 5 K [assumed].
- Heat pump: COP = eps (T_s + a)/((T_s + a) - (T_c - a)); source heat Q_sink (COP - 1)/COP; work Q_sink/COP.
All three are second-law consistent by construction: their COPs never exceed the reversible limit of the stream temperatures.

**Heat rejection.** Outlet temperature = dry-bulb (dry cooler) or wet-bulb (tower, from CoolProp humid-air) plus approach (8 K, 5 K [assumed]); fans use 1.5 % of the heat rejected [assumed]. Make-up water of a tower is the upper bound Q / h_fg(T_out).

**IT load.** Consumes P_it of electricity; a fraction f of it is carried by the chilled-water loop, the rest is rejected directly to ambient.

**Sizing.** The prime mover (air flow of a turbine, rated power of an engine) is scaled by secant iteration on full plant solves until net electricity (generation minus parasitics plus ORC output) equals 1.03 P_it.

## 2. Exergy accounting
Dead state (T0, P0, ambient air composition). Physical exergy e_ph = (h - h0) - T0 (s - s0), with h0, s0 at (T0, P0) for the same composition. Chemical exergy of a gas, e_ch = sum_i y_i eps_i + R T0 sum_i y_i ln y_i, with eps_i = -R T0 ln y_env,i for species in the environment (so ambient air has zero exergy) and eps_CH4 = -dG + eps_CO2 + 2 eps_H2O - 2 eps_O2, where dG is the Gibbs energy change of CH4 + 2 O2 -> CO2 + 2 H2O(g) at (T0, 1 atm). Liquids carry no chemical exergy. Component destruction E_D = sum_in n e - sum_out n e - W_out; heat dissipated to ambient carries no exergy. Plant closure: sum E_D = sum_ext-in n e - sum_ext-out n e - sum W. Exergy efficiency eta_II = (W_net + exergy rise of delivered steam, heat and chilled water) / fuel exergy.

## 3. Verifiers (the reward signal)
- V1 mass balance per component, tolerance 1e-6 of the largest flow. V2 energy balance, 1e-6 of (|W_net| + Q_fuel + 1 MW).
- V3a E_D >= -1e-5 E_fuel for every component. V3b closure within 1e-6 E_fuel.
- V4a minimum approach temperature >= 0 K in every heat exchanger (hard); V4b >= 1 K (soft).
- V5 eta_II in [0, 1]; power efficiency below 1 - T0/T_max; ORC efficiency below 1 - T_cond/T_hot; (V5d) the COP of every chiller and heat pump at or below the reversible COP of its stream temperatures.
- V6 parameter and state ranges (efficiencies in (0, 1], pressure ratio > 1, pressure drops in [0, 0.3], temperatures 150 to 2500 K, pressures 1 kPa to 100 MPa, direction of pressure change across compressors, pumps and turbines).
- V7 every number with a unit in a reasoning text must match a solver or plant value within 0.5 % (3 % for predicted Design quantities).
- V8 site requirements: IT load within 1 %, electricity balance (when grid import is not allowed), cooling >= 99 % of the load, heat, and a water limit.

## 4. Tasks
**Repair.** A valid plant is broken by one of five fault types (unit slip, sign flip, efficiency above 1, swapped ports, wrong fluid). The model reads the plant, the site and the solver/verifier result, and answers with a diagnosis and at most six edits (`SET`, `REWIRE`, `FLUID`) on existing numbers, endpoints and fluids. Splits are structural: unseen fault types (sign flip, swapped ports) and unseen topologies (steam-ORC plant, gas engine) are held out.

**Design.** Input: a site (IT load, chilled-water load, ambient temperature and humidity, coolant temperatures, optional heat demand and water limit). Output: prime mover, cooling, heat rejection, bottoming cycle (none or ORC with one of three fluids), district-heat source when needed, and continuous parameters. Objective: least fuel among valid plants (services are fixed by the site). The reference is the best of enumeration plus a Nelder-Mead polish (60 evaluations on the best two configurations). Held-out regimes: scale (>= 45 MW), climate (>= 32 C), chilled fraction (>= 0.85).

**Trace-weighted fuel (Design).** A load trace is split into quantile bins of IT power; the design is evaluated at each bin's median load and coolant temperatures (and ambient temperature when the trace has it), weighted by the time in the bin. The plant is re-sized to every bin, so efficiency does not depend on load: ideal part-load. The result is a lower bound on fuel, used to compare designs, not to predict a bill. Bins in which the design is infeasible are reported by time share and excluded from the energy.

**Modular gensets.** To remove the ideal part-load assumption without inventing efficiency curves, the prime mover is split into n identical units that run at rated output or not at all. Installed capacity is the largest bin need times a margin; the bin's units online are the smallest count that covers its need; the plant is solved with that many units' output, the surplus electricity is curtailed and its fuel is wasted. The modularity penalty is the fuel above the ideal part-load fuel. Because fuel scales with generation for both prime movers, the penalty depends on the load-trace shape and the unit count and, to first order, not on the technology (parasitic loads that do not scale with generation, such as electric chillers or a heat pump, break the tie slightly). Not modelled: unit efficiency depending on unit size, minimum run time, start-up and ramp limits, capacity limits of heat-recovery equipment and chillers, standby units beyond the capacity margin.

**Audit.** Input: a solved plant with an energy-level summary (exergy destruction hidden). Output: the ranking of exergy destroyers, one allowed set-point change, and the predicted change in fuel input after re-sizing the prime mover to the same net electricity. Allowed changes: pressure ratio, combustor outlet temperature and pressure drop, loss fraction, heat-exchanger pressure drop, ORC pinch and flow position, ORC pump pressure, fan fraction, engine air excess and loss fraction, chiller water rise, each within [0.8, 1.25] of the current value; component efficiencies and tear-closing temperatures are excluded.

**Ledger repair.** The exergy ledger of a solved plant is a list of arithmetic lines: `T0 = <ambient K>`; `E[x] = m[x]*(h[x] - T0*s[x] - g0[x] + ech[x])/1e6` for every stream (g0 = h0 - T0 s0 is the dead-state reference of the stream's fluid); `ED[c] = sum E[in] - sum E[out] - W[c]` for every component (the work term only for components that deliver or consume work). Thirteen fault types are injected into one or two lines: in stream lines (missing flow factor, missing chemical term, /1e3 for /1e6, a sign on the T0 term, another stream's reference, the number 25 for T0), in component lines (flipped sign, dropped stream, dropped work term, factor 1000 or 0.001, factor 0.1/10/2/0.5, wrong stream) and in the constant (T0 in degrees Celsius, T0 = 273.15). The model sees the ledger, the topology, the ambient temperature, the stream values and the evaluated result (every ED, every E, the closure residual) and answers with up to four corrected lines. Lines are evaluated by a restricted arithmetic evaluator; nothing is executed. Measured on 400 generated rows: faults in component lines always break the closure; faults in stream lines break it only 10 to 31 percent of the time because errors in internal streams cancel in the sum, but produce a negative ED in 50 to 89 percent; the two T0 faults are visible in only 45 to 46 percent; overall 86 percent show at least one second-law symptom, and parameter-range checks (V6) see none. A program that rebuilds every line from the topology solves every row by construction, so the task measures applying that rule from text and localising the fault.

## 5. Rewards
Weights in brackets. Repair [0.5, 2, 1, 1, 0.5, 0.5]: format; validity (1 if the plant solves and passes all hard checks, else 0.5 x fraction of checks passed); site requirements; exact restoration when the fault is deducible; minimality 1/(1 + extra edits beyond the minimum); grounding of the diagnosis. Design [0.5, 2, 2, 0.5]: format; validity (1 valid, 0.2 built but invalid); quality 1 - (fuel - fuel_ref)/(0.1 fuel_ref) clipped to [0, 1.05]; grounding of predicted numbers. Ledger [0.5, 1, 0.5, 2.5, 0.5, 0.5]: format; closure 1 when |residual| <= 1e-6 of the fuel exergy, falling to 0 at 1 percent; share of components with ED >= 0; exactness, (faulty lines that now evaluate to the true value - correct lines now broken) / faulty lines; minimality 1/(1 + extra lines) once everything is correct; diagnosis numbers (MW) must appear in the prompt. Audit [0.5, 1.5, 1, 2, 1, 0.5]: format; nDCG@3 of the ranking with the prime mover removed; validity of the change; gain = saved fuel / best allowed saving, clipped to [0, 1.05]; prediction 1 - |pred - actual| / max(|actual|, 0.2 % of fuel); grounding.

## 6. Training
Qwen3.5-4B (decoder-only). Stage 1, SFT on the verifier-gated corpus (ground-truth paraphrases, architecture corpus, accepted teacher answers, failure-memory notes), LoRA r = 16, alpha = 32, all linear layers, learning rate 2e-4, 2 epochs, prompts rendered with the tokenizer's generation prompt exactly as at GRPO and evaluation time. Stage 2, GRPO from the merged SFT model, LoRA r = 8, alpha = 16, learning rate 5e-5, group size 4, temperature 1.0, 320 completion tokens, DAPO-style loss without a KL term, group-normalised rewards, truncated completions masked. Evaluation is greedy; Repair and Design allow up to three attempts with solver feedback, and cost is counted in solver calls or design evaluations.

## 6a. Reward diagnostics
Before any GPU time, each reward set is probed with simulated policies that return the expert answer with probability 1 - e and a damaged answer otherwise (extreme numbers, truncation, duplication, deleted slices, broken syntax, rarely plain noise). For groups of 8 the diagnostic reports the mean reward, the spread inside groups, and the share of groups whose rewards are all equal. Such groups carry no gradient under group-normalised advantages, so they indicate the error rate below which GRPO stops being informative for a task.

## 6b. Evaluation protocol and statistics
Models are evaluated greedily on held-out splits. Repair and Design allow up to three attempts with solver feedback; the Design anytime protocol additionally reports the fuel of every valid design to the model and records the best gap to the reference after each design evaluation, so that the model can be compared with enumeration, random search and enumerate-then-polish at equal evaluation budgets. Binary outcomes carry Wilson 95 % intervals. Two runs on the same examples are compared with an exact McNemar test (binary metrics) or a paired bootstrap interval plus a sign-flip permutation test (other metrics), with Holm correction across all comparisons reported together. Per-example results are saved with each report.

## 7. Settings that were assumed rather than measured
Engine electrical efficiency 0.43 and loss fraction 0.10 to 0.20; turbine efficiencies 0.86 to 0.88; chiller reversible-limit fractions and the absorption COP ceiling; heat-pump approach 5 K; rejector approaches and fan fraction; ORC fluid temperature limits and 333 K minimum stack temperature; fraction of IT heat on the chilled loop. Design and Audit outcomes depend on these; they are not fitted to data.
