# AGENTS.md: energy-plant tasks

You work on simulated plants for AI data centres (gas prime mover, cooling, heat recovery). A solver and physics verifiers judge every answer, so only claims that the solver would reproduce are useful.

## Conventions
- SI units: K, Pa, W, kg/s. Efficiencies, second-law fractions (`eps`) and pressure-drop fractions (`dp*`) are fractions, never percent. Temperatures are kelvin, never degrees Celsius.
- A plant is a graph. `C <id> <type> {params}` is a component; `S <id> <from> -> <to> <fluid> {extras}` is a stream. Endpoints are `<component>.<port>` or `ext:<name>` (a boundary).
- Fluid classes: gas streams are `CGAM_AIR`, `FLUE`, `CH4`; every other fluid is a liquid or two-phase CoolProp fluid (`Water`, `R245fa`, `Cyclopentane`, `Toluene`). A port accepts only its own class.
- A stream whose source is a component but which carries `fixed` values is a tear stream: it closes a loop, and its fixed T and P must equal what the producing component computes. Never change one side of a tear without the other.
- Flows that a component solves itself must not be specified (engine air and fuel, chiller generator and rejection flows, IT coolant flow, heat-pump sink flow).

## What the verifiers check
V1 mass balance, V2 energy balance, V3 exergy: closure and no negative exergy destruction in any component, V4 no temperature cross (min approach below 0 K fails), V5 second-law and Carnot bounds, V6 unit and range checks, V7 numbers in your text must match the solver, V8 site requirements (IT load served, no grid import unless allowed, cooling, heat, water limit).

## Reading a solver error
- E_PROP_RANGE: a value is non-positive, a unit slip, or outside the property data (a pressure of 20 Pa, a temperature of 1226 K entered as 1226 degC).
- E_TEMP_CROSS: heat cannot flow as specified: hot and cold inlets swapped, a duty larger than the hot stream can supply, or an inlet colder than the achievable outlet.
- E_NOT_CONVERGED: a tear stream does not close, or an outlet temperature is not reachable.
- E_FLUID_MISMATCH: a stream's fluid class does not fit the port it feeds.
- E_DISCONNECTED, E_OVER_SPECIFIED, E_UNDER_SPECIFIED: a port has no stream or two, or a value that should be solved is given, or one is missing.
- Solved but failing a check: read the check name and its detail; V6 names the offending parameter.

# Absorption chiller

<!-- AUTO:components -->
### absorption_chiller
Ports: gen_in (liquid), gen_out (liquid), evap_in (liquid), evap_out (liquid), rej_in (liquid), rej_out (liquid)
Params: `T_chw_out`: chilled-water outlet temperature (K), required; `T_gen_out`: generator outlet temperature (K), required; `dT_rej`: heat-rejection water temperature rise (K), required; `eps`: fraction of the reversible COP (-), 0.27; `COP_max`: first-law COP ceiling (-), 0.8
<!-- /AUTO -->

## Behaviour
- COP = min(`eps` x reversible COP, `COP_max`). The reversible COP uses log-mean temperatures of the generator, evaporator and rejection streams. Measured single-effect LiBr/H2O units run at COP 0.55 to 0.70; `COP_max` 0.8 is the model's first-law ceiling.
- The chilled-water load fixes the generator duty (load / COP). The generator flow and the rejection flow are solved: do not specify them.
- Hot-water loop: heat exchanger `cold_out` feeds `gen_in`; `gen_out` returns to the exchanger through a tear stream whose fixed T must equal `T_gen_out`.
- Driving temperature (generator inlet) must exceed the rejection temperature, and the rejection temperature must exceed the evaporator temperature, otherwise E_TEMP_CROSS.
- A hotter rejection loop (dry cooler, hot climate) lowers the reversible COP; the cap hides this only while the reversible limit is far above it.

## Typical faults
- `eps` above 1 or `COP_max` far above 1 breaks V6 or V3 (a machine beyond the reversible limit shows negative exergy destruction).
- `T_chw_out` not below the inlet, `T_gen_out` not below the generator inlet: E_PROP_RANGE.
- `T_gen_out` edited without the tear stream's fixed T: E_NOT_CONVERGED.
- Swapped `gen_in` and `rej_in` streams: the generator sees cooling water, E_PROP_RANGE.

## State: constant versus changing
Fixed: refrigerant pair, `T_chw_out` and the loop fluids; generator, chilled and rejection flows are solved. Generator heat drives the cycle; temperatures and duties change within `eps`/`COP_max`, and `dT_rej` sets the rejection temperature.

# District heat: heat pump or exhaust

<!-- AUTO:components -->
### heat_pump
Ports: src_in (liquid), src_out (liquid), sink_in (liquid), sink_out (liquid)
Params: `T_sink_out`: sink outlet temperature (K), required; `eps`: fraction of the Lorenz COP (-), 0.5; `approach_K`: evaporator and condenser approach (K), 5.0; `Q_sink`: heat duty to deliver (W); sizes the source cooling, one of Q_sink or dT_src; `dT_src`: source-side temperature drop (K), one of Q_sink or dT_src

### recuperator
Ports: cold_in (any), cold_out (any), hot_in (any), hot_out (any)
Params: `T_cold_out`: cold-side outlet temperature (K), required; `dp_cold`: cold-side pressure-drop fraction (-), 0.0; `dp_hot`: hot-side pressure-drop fraction (-), 0.0; `useful_heat`: flag: duty counts as delivered heat, False
<!-- /AUTO -->

## Behaviour
- Exhaust route: a `recuperator` with `useful_heat = true` heats district water on the exhaust, after the chiller's own heat exchanger. Its duty counts as delivered heat. The district-water flow is fixed by the demand and the supply/return temperatures.
- Heat-pump route: the heat pump sits in series on the chiller rejection water (before the heat rejector). With `Q_sink` it delivers exactly that heat and cools the source as far as needed; the sink flow is solved. The heat pump uses electricity: `W = Q_sink - Q_source`, a parasitic load that raises fuel use.
- COP = `eps` x Lorenz COP on refrigerant-side temperatures (stream log-mean temperatures moved outward by `approach_K`). It needs a sink hotter than the source and COP above 1.
- The heat pump needs a dry or wet rejection loop (not an ideal boundary). If it cools the rejection water below what the rejector can reach, the rejector raises E_TEMP_CROSS.
- The exhaust route is cheaper in fuel when the exhaust still has heat after the other recoveries; the heat pump route leaves the exhaust free for an ORC.

## Typical faults
- Sink outlet not hotter than sink inlet: E_PROP_RANGE.
- Neither `Q_sink` nor `dT_src` given: E_UNDER_SPECIFIED (if both are given, `Q_sink` is used).
- District flow specified on a heat-pump sink (it is solved): E_OVER_SPECIFIED.


SKILL heat-rejector: Use when a plant contains a heat_rejector (dry cooler or evaporative cooling tower) closing a cooling-water loop.
# Heat rejector

<!-- AUTO:components -->
### heat_rejector
Ports: in (liquid), out (liquid)
Params: `kind`: 'dry' (dry cooler) or 'wet' (evaporative tower), dry; `approach_K`: approach to dry-bulb or wet-bulb (K), required; `fan_frac`: fan electricity as a fraction of heat rejected (-), 0.015
<!-- /AUTO -->

## Behaviour
- Outlet temperature = dry-bulb (`kind` dry) or wet-bulb (`kind` wet) plus `approach_K`. Wet-bulb comes from ambient temperature and relative humidity, so a tower cools colder than a dry cooler, most of all in dry air.
- Fans use `fan_frac` of the heat rejected as electricity (a parasitic load).
- A wet tower consumes make-up water. The model reports an upper bound: all heat rejected as latent heat. A site with a water limit rules wet towers out (V8e).
- The loop is closed by a tear stream: the stream leaving the rejector carries `fixed` T and P, and its T must equal the rejector outlet temperature. Changing `approach_K` or `kind` without changing that fixed T gives E_NOT_CONVERGED.
- The inlet must be warmer than the achievable outlet, otherwise E_TEMP_CROSS.
- Heat leaves the system at ambient: it counts as exergy destroyed in the rejector although it carries little exergy.

## Typical faults
- `kind` not `dry` or `wet`, `approach_K` of 0 or negative, `fan_frac` above 0.1.

## State: constant versus changing
Fixed: water flow and ambient T/P; outlet T = wet/dry bulb + `approach_K`, fan uses `fan_frac` of rejected heat, and the tear stream's fixed T must match. Heat leaves near ambient: large duty, small exergy loss.
